What could knock Disney off course, and what can it do about it today?

AI players compete as Disney and its rivals over ten years. A referee scores every move, and an analyst recommends what Disney could do now. Follow steps 1 to 3. How this works

Built on real data: Disney's FY26 results and FY27 guidance, plus each rival's latest reported financials. Everything after today is simulated, so treat results as scenarios, not forecasts. Don't enter confidential information.

How the game works

Each game covers FY27 to FY36 in five two-year turns. Each turn, Disney's player moves first, the rival players respond, and the referee works out what each move costs and how it changes Disney's profit in each business. At the start of each game, two newcomers are cast to fit your scenario: one real company and one invented one. A "rest of the market" player stands in for smaller rivals. Every player is briefed on its company's real finances as of late 2026. When the games end, the analyst reads every move and writes up the results.

Instant check: the next decade, simulated 1,000 times in a second No AI, no wait

The war game plays out the next decade a few times in full detail, with AI players making specific moves. The instant check plays it out 1,000 times with simple rules instead: each year, outside shocks and rival pressure hit at the odds you set in step 1, and the math adds up Disney's profit. It uses the same assumptions and shock list as the war game, but with no AI players it can't invent moves or recommend actions.

Use it to: see which threats cost Disney the most across many futures, the range of profit outcomes, how the six strategies compare, and how the same rules would have done from 2019 against what actually happened.

      Sources and assumptions

      Real numbers. The starting point is Disney's FY26 segment operating income from its filings: Experiences $11.2 billion, Entertainment $5.4 billion, Sports $3.1 billion, $19.7 billion in total. "Profit" on this page means segment operating income. Rival figures come from each company's latest reports.

      Assumptions. Growth starts at Disney's FY27 guidance (about 8% a year) and slows to about 5% by FY31. Step 1 sets how often shocks hit and how hard rivals push. These are estimates, meant to be replaced with a strategy team's own.

      Track record. To test the model, it was run as if it were 2019, using only what was known then. Its list of possible shocks included 10 of the 11 big events that actually hit Disney through FY26, including the pandemic. It underestimated how quickly Disney's profit bounced back afterward.

      AI. Rival players run on Claude Haiku 5.5, Disney and the referee on Claude Sonnet 5.5, the analyst on Claude Opus 5.5. The code is on GitHub.
